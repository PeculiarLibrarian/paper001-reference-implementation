# Executive Intelligence Framework
Version: 1.0.0

---

# Purpose

The Executive Intelligence Framework defines the constitutional process by which the Peculiar Library transforms institutional knowledge into executive-grade decision support.

The framework is independent of implementation technology and applies uniformly across all Practice Areas.

---

# Objective

Produce evidence-based, explainable, and actionable intelligence for executive leaders, boards of directors, regulators, and institutional investors.

# Stage 1 — Executive Question

Every engagement begins with one or more executive questions.

Examples include:

- Should we invest?
- What is the principal institutional risk?
- What creates competitive advantage?
- Which strategic initiative delivers the greatest value?

Executive questions define analytical scope.

---

# Stage 2 — Evidence Acquisition

Evidence is collected from authoritative institutional sources.

Examples:

- Financial statements
- Regulatory filings
- Annual reports
- Legal instruments
- Internal governance documents
- Market intelligence
- Industry data

---

# Stage 3 — Evidence Validation

Evidence shall satisfy:

- authenticity
- completeness
- consistency
- provenance
- timeliness

Invalid evidence shall not participate in reasoning.

---

# Stage 4 — Knowledge Integration

Validated evidence is transformed into interoperable semantic knowledge.

This includes:

- entity resolution
- ontology alignment
- taxonomy alignment
- semantic normalization
- graph integration

---

# Stage 5 — Semantic Reasoning

Reasoning derives institutional knowledge that is not explicitly present in source documents.

Reasoning shall remain explainable.

---

# Stage 6 — Findings

Findings represent validated institutional conclusions.

Every finding shall reference supporting evidence.

---

# Stage 7 — Risk and Opportunity Assessment

Each finding shall be evaluated for:

- strategic significance
- operational impact
- financial impact
- governance impact
- regulatory impact

---

# Stage 8 — Recommendations

Recommendations shall:

- address identified findings
- mitigate risks
- exploit opportunities
- support executive objectives

Recommendations shall never exist without supporting findings.

---

# Stage 9 — Executive Decision Support

Recommendations are organized into executive alternatives.

Decision support includes:

- trade-offs
- assumptions
- uncertainty
- expected outcomes

---

# Stage 10 — Executive Intelligence Report

Executive reports shall contain:

- Executive Summary
- Strategic Context
- Evidence Base
- Findings
- Risks
- Opportunities
- Recommendations
- Decision Alternatives
- Expected Outcomes
- Traceability Appendix

---

# Stage 11 — Institutional Learning

Every engagement produces institutional knowledge that becomes reusable evidence for future engagements.

The library continuously improves through accumulated knowledge.

---

# Constitutional Principles

Executive intelligence shall be:

- evidence-based
- explainable
- reproducible
- semantically interoperable
- institutionally traceable
- strategically relevant

No executive conclusion shall exist without complete semantic provenance.
