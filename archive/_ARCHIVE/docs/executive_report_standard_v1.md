# Executive Report Standard v1.0.0

## Purpose

This standard defines the structure, quality requirements and governing
principles for every executive report produced by the Peculiar Librarian.

Executive reports are decision-support documents.

They do not make decisions.

They provide evidence-based strategic intelligence that enables executives,
boards and investors to make informed decisions.

---

# Audience

Executive reports are intended for:

• Boards of Directors

• Chief Executive Officers

• Chief Financial Officers

• Chief Strategy Officers

• Investment Committees

• Institutional Investors

• Regulators

• Professional Advisors

---

# Principles

Every report SHALL be:

• Evidence-based

• Objective

• Traceable

• Reproducible

• Material

• MECE

• Explainable

• Executive-focused

---

# Required Sections

Every executive report SHALL contain the following sections.

1. Executive Summary

A concise overview of the engagement.

This section highlights only the most material findings.

---

2. Executive Question

The strategic question addressed by the engagement.

---

3. Scope

Definition of:

• organisation

• reporting period

• geography

• practice areas

• assumptions

• constraints

---

4. Methodology

Describe:

• PADI methodology

• semantic reasoning approach

• evidence sources

• validation process

---

5. Strategic Context

Provide sufficient business context for executive interpretation.

---

6. Executive Findings

Each finding SHALL include:

• description

• supporting evidence

• confidence

• materiality

• provenance

---

7. Strategic Risks

Material risks identified during reasoning.

Each risk SHALL include:

• likelihood

• potential impact

• supporting evidence

---

8. Strategic Opportunities

Material opportunities identified during reasoning.

Each opportunity SHALL include:

• expected value

• supporting evidence

• strategic implications

---

9. Scenario Analysis

Where applicable, evaluate multiple strategic scenarios.

Each scenario SHALL identify:

• assumptions

• risks

• trade-offs

• likely outcomes

---

10. Recommendations

Each recommendation SHALL include:

• rationale

• expected benefits

• risks

• dependencies

• implementation considerations

Recommendations SHALL always reference supporting findings.

---

11. Confidence Assessment

Overall engagement confidence.

Confidence SHALL be derived from:

• evidence completeness

• semantic consistency

• reasoning coverage

• source quality

---

12. Provenance Appendix

Every executive statement SHALL be traceable through the evidence chain.

---

# Report Quality Standards

A report SHALL NOT contain:

• unsupported claims

• speculative conclusions

• unverifiable recommendations

• missing provenance

• duplicate findings

• contradictory statements

---

# Executive Writing Principles

Reports SHALL be:

clear

concise

precise

objective

professionally structured

strategically relevant

executive readable

# Definition of Completion

An executive report is complete only when every conclusion can be reproduced
from validated institutional knowledge.
