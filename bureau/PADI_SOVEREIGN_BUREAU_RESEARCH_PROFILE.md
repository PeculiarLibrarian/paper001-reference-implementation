# Research Identity

S. M. Gitandu is an Information Scientist whose research investigates the engineering of trustworthy knowledge systems through Semantic Interoperability Engineering.

His work is founded upon the conviction that Information Science provides the intellectual foundations necessary for building explainable, interoperable, and governed intelligent systems.

Rather than treating Artificial Intelligence solely as a computational discipline, his research approaches intelligence as a knowledge organization problem.

---

# Academic Background

His academic training in Information Science and Librarianship emphasized:

- Knowledge Organization
- Metadata
- Classification
- Cataloguing
- Authority Control
- Information Retrieval
- Information Governance
- Preservation of Institutional Knowledge

These principles continue to guide his research into semantic technologies.

---

# Research Philosophy

His research is guided by a central principle:

> **The world does not suffer from a lack of information.  
> The world suffers from a lack of semantic interoperability.**

Organizations possess extensive information resources.

However, without shared semantic foundations, institutional knowledge becomes fragmented, inconsistent, and difficult to preserve.

His research therefore focuses on engineering systems that preserve meaning before computation.

# Research Contribution

His work proposes that Semantic Interoperability Engineering represents the computational evolution of Information Science.

Rather than replacing the principles of librarianship, semantic technologies operationalize them within modern computational environments.

Within this perspective:

- Classification evolves into ontology engineering.
- Authority control evolves into semantic identity.
- Metadata evolves into canonical representation.
- Cataloguing evolves into knowledge graph engineering.
- Knowledge organization evolves into semantic interoperability.

The intellectual tradition remains continuous.

His research ultimately aims to strengthen institutional intelligence through the engineering of shared meaning.
