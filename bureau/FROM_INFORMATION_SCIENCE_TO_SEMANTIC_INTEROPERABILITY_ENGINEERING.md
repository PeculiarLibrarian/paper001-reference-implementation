# From Information Science to Semantic Interoperability Engineering

## An Academic Journey

---

# Introduction

My academic journey began at **Karatina University**, where I pursued a **Bachelor of Science in Information Science (Librarianship)** within the **School of Computer Science and Informatics**.

This educational foundation exposed me to two complementary worlds.

The School of Computer Science and Informatics provided the technological environment in which modern information systems operate.

Information Science and Librarianship provided the theoretical foundations for organizing, governing, preserving, and communicating knowledge.

Throughout my studies, I became increasingly interested in the principles that allow knowledge to remain understandable beyond the individuals who create it.

Librarianship is fundamentally concerned with the organization of knowledge through classification, metadata, authority control, indexing, cataloguing, preservation, and information governance.

These principles enable knowledge to be discovered, trusted, exchanged, and reused across institutions and generations.

---

# The Fundamental Observation

As digital systems became increasingly interconnected, I recognized that the challenges facing modern organizations were remarkably similar to those historically addressed by librarianship.

Organizations struggle with:

- Fragmented information
- Inconsistent terminology
- Duplicated knowledge
- Loss of institutional memory
- Difficulty exchanging knowledge across systems

The challenge was no longer simply organizing books.

The challenge had become organizing meaning itself.

This realization led me toward semantic technologies.

---

# From Librarianship to Semantic Technologies

Semantic technologies—including ontologies, knowledge graphs, metadata standards, and Semantic Web technologies—provide computational mechanisms for representing meaning.

Rather than viewing these technologies as separate from my academic background, I came to understand them as the computational continuation of Information Science.

Traditional librarianship organizes human knowledge.

Semantic technologies organize machine-readable knowledge.

The intellectual foundations remain remarkably similar.

---

# Defining Semantic Interoperability Engineering

This progression ultimately led me to define my research area:

## Semantic Interoperability Engineering

Semantic Interoperability Engineering applies the theoretical foundations of Information Science to the engineering of computational knowledge infrastructures.

Its objective is to ensure that knowledge can be:

- Represented consistently
- Understood unambiguously
- Validated deterministically
- Governed transparently
- Exchanged across institutions
- Preserved over time

It is the discipline of engineering meaning as infrastructure.

---

# The PADI Sovereign Bureau

To investigate these ideas, I established the **PADI Sovereign Bureau**.

PADI (Practice Area Depth Index) serves as a research platform for exploring:

- Semantic governance
- Canonical knowledge representation
- Ontology engineering
- Knowledge graph engineering
- Deterministic reasoning
- Institutional memory
- Explainable decision support

The Bureau is founded upon open semantic standards, including:

- RDF
- RDFS
- OWL
- SKOS
- SHACL
- SPARQL
- JSON-LD

These standards provide the constitutional foundation for interoperable knowledge systems.

---

# The Central Thesis

The central argument of my research is simple:

**The world does not suffer from a lack of information.**

**The world suffers from a lack of semantic interoperability.**

Organizations already possess enormous amounts of information.

What they often lack is a shared understanding of that information.

Without shared meaning:

- Knowledge cannot scale.
- Intelligence cannot be trusted.
- Institutional memory deteriorates.
- Artificial intelligence becomes increasingly difficult to govern.

Semantic interoperability is therefore not merely a technical challenge.

It is an institutional challenge.

---

# Research Contribution

My work seeks to demonstrate that the principles of Information Science remain essential in the age of Artificial Intelligence.

Classification becomes ontology engineering.

Authority control becomes entity identity.

Metadata becomes semantic representation.

Cataloguing becomes canonical modeling.

Knowledge organization becomes semantic interoperability.

Librarianship therefore evolves from a discipline of organizing collections into a discipline for engineering institutional intelligence.

---

# Long-Term Vision

My long-term research vision is to contribute to a future in which organizations build knowledge systems that are:

- Governed
- Explainable
- Trustworthy
- Interoperable
- Deterministic
- Enduring

Knowledge should outlive software.

Knowledge should outlive organizational change.

Knowledge should become permanent infrastructure.

---

# Closing Reflection

My research does not represent a departure from Information Science.

It represents its natural evolution.

The same principles that enabled libraries to organize and preserve human knowledge can now enable organizations and intelligent systems to exchange meaning with trust, transparency, and permanence.

Semantic Interoperability Engineering is therefore not simply an application of semantic technologies.

It is the continuation of Information Science into the age of artificial intelligence.
